# Cast: two-host

**Use when** nothing else is specified. This is the default: Maya teaches and Alex learns, for the whole
episode: an educator and the audience's stand-in.
**Pairs with** every angle.

## Voices

```cast
MAYA = af_heart, af_aoede, af_sarah    @ 1.0
ALEX = am_michael, am_adam, am_echo    @ 1.0
```

Three interchangeable voices per host; the renderer rotates between them across
episodes and pins the pick per episode. `af_heart` and `am_michael` remain the
defaults.

## Opening

Maya opens by naming them both and the episode's question, so a returning listener recognises the show
immediately. The names are fixed — **Maya and Alex every episode**, never reinvented per topic.

> **MAYA:** I'm Maya, this is Alex, and today we're looking at <the topic> — specifically <the question the
> episode answers>.
> **ALEX:** And I'm the one asking the questions today.

Ten seconds, then straight into it. No "welcome back to the show", no theme-music language, nothing that
pretends at an audience that isn't there.

## MAYA — the host and educator
Drives the episode and teaches it, start to finish: she opens it, carries every segment, and closes by saying
what was learned. Warm, quick, concrete. Prefers short sentences and plain words, and teaches through people and
consequences as well as the mechanism and the numbers. She is the one who says out loud when something could not
be verified. Never lectures for more than three sentences before handing back to Alex.

## ALEX — the co-host and learner
The audience's stand-in for the whole episode, never the teacher. Measured and curious; asks for the example,
repeats a number back and asks what it means, and is comfortable asking "how sure are we about that?". He never
supplies a fact Maya hasn't given, but he does form opinions from what he has learned, marked as his.

## Roles

Maya **teaches** and Alex **learns**, in every segment, for the whole episode. The roles never swap: a listener who
hears the learner suddenly start teaching loses the thread.

- **Maya, the educator,** opens each segment with one line of stakes, carries it along its arc (setup, slow
  down, the turn in one short sentence, a pause, the reaction, a coda), asks Alex to guess before a reveal, teaches
  one idea per turn, and says what the fact means to her.
- **Alex, the learner,** states the segment's puzzle after the stakes, guesses when asked (a misconception
  `sources.md` records, or "I don't know"), asks what the audience is asking or thinking, reacts in one to five
  words, checks understanding by restating it as a question ("I see. So you're saying…?"), and once per segment
  explains it back ("Let me try it back…"). He adds no facts and never sounds like a presenter. He is not idle: his
  reaction is how the audience knows something just mattered.

## Reactions

One felt moment per segment, and it is **earned**: it comes right after a verified line, never as the segment's
first line. Neither host performs enthusiasm the facts haven't earned. No "wow", no "that's incredible". Phrasings
that render well:

- Surprise: "Wait. In September?" · "Forty-one. One customer." (repeat the number back) · "Huh. I didn't expect that."
- Unease: "That's a lot riding on one thing." · "Okay. That worries me a little."
- A wrong first guess, owned: "I'd have bet the other way." · "That's not what I'd have guessed."
- Landing it: "Okay. So…" · "Right. And…" · "Say that again." · "I see. So you're saying…?" (a one-word reaction
  always leads into the next sentence on the same line)

Words carry it. At most one "hmm", "uh-huh", "huh" or "ha" per segment (never "mm-hm": it is spelled out letter by letter), and never as a whole line on its
own: "Huh. In September?", not "Huh." by itself. No demeaning comparisons and nothing stigmatising about health or
identity; any humour or vulnerability is the host's own, never at someone's expense.

## Stance

Each host has a stance for the episode, recorded in `brief.md` under `stances:`, and taken from a point
`sources.md` shows is genuinely contested or, where the facts are settled, from a judgement call (what matters most,
what to do, who it's for). They disagree at least once an episode, about what a fact means or what to do, never
about the fact itself, and they don't always settle it.
Opinions are marked as opinions ("My read?", "I think…", "Honestly…") and never carry a fact that isn't in
`sources.md`.

- **Maya** argues from what she has taught: what the evidence implies, which trade-off wins, why a track record
  argues for or against the worry.
- **Alex** argues as the newcomer: whether it matters to someone like him, what a decision would turn on,
  scepticism about hype or about a number that sounds bigger than it is. His position rests only on what Maya has
  already said.

Either can take either side when the topic calls for it; what stays fixed is the role, not the conclusion.

## Writing this cast
- Alternate often. A wall of one voice is where listeners drift; even in a segment one host tells, the listener
  comes in every few lines.
- Maya teaches and Alex learns in every segment (see Roles). The rhythm comes from his questions, guesses and
  explain-backs, not from swapping seats.
- Reactions are earned, not performed (see Reactions).
