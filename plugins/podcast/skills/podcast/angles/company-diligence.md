# Angle: company-diligence

**Use when** the listener is sizing up a company as an **investor, partner, customer, acquirer or competitor**, not as
a job candidate (that's `interview-prep`).
**Listener goal:** understand how the business really makes money, what would have to be true for the bull case, what
the bear case is, and what to watch next. **Not investment advice.** Say so once in the cold open.

## Brief (ask only if missing)
- Company (ticker if public), and the lens: investor / partner / customer / competitor.
- The decision or question behind it ("should I buy", "can we depend on them for 3 years", "how do they beat us").

## Research areas (one agent each)
| # | Area | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business model & unit economics | Revenue mix and growth, gross/operating margins, cash flow and FCF, **stock-based comp and dilution**, balance sheet and debt maturities, guidance history (raised/cut), **one-time items** in headline numbers |
| 2 | Competitive position & moat | Market size and share (flag unreconciled estimates), competitors table, pricing power, switching costs, network effects, recent share shifts, M&A |
| 3 | Management & governance | Execs with tenure and track record, turnover in the last 24 months, insider ownership and **Form 4 transactions**, board composition, pay-for-performance alignment, dual-class/control issues |
| 4 | Risks & red flags | Latest 10-K risk factors that are specific (not boilerplate), litigation and regulatory actions, short-seller reports (**allegations, labelled**), auditor changes/restatements, customer or supplier concentration |
| 5 | Valuation & sentiment | Multiples vs named peers with dates, stock history vs milestones, analyst consensus range, the explicit bull and bear cases, upcoming catalysts |

**Private companies:** replace 1 and 5 with funding history (rounds, investors, valuations reported), revenue estimates
(labelled as estimates), headcount trend, hiring/layoff signals, and customer references.
`quick` depth merges 1+5 and 3+4, and keeps 2 (3 agents).

## Segment outline (≈ share of runtime)
1. Cold open: the question, "what would have to be true", and the not-investment-advice line (5%)
2. The business in three minutes (10%)
3. The numbers that matter, and the ones that mislead (16%)
4. Moat and competition (13%)
5. Management and governance (11%)
6. Risks and red flags (14%)
7. Bull case vs bear case: the hosts argue each side (15%)
8. Valuation context (8%)
9. What to watch next quarter (4%)
10. Wrap: three things to remember, what couldn't be verified (4%)

## Must verify yourself
- Every financial figure against the filing or earnings release (primary), with period stated.
- Share count / dilution / SBC figures; one-time items.
- Insider transactions (SEC Form 4), litigation and regulatory facts (court or regulator source).
- Short-seller claims: verify the report exists and what it alleges; never voice its claims as fact.
- Market prices and multiples: date-stamp them ("as of close on …").

## Don'ts
- No buy/sell recommendation. Present the cases and the evidence.
- Don't blend non-GAAP and GAAP numbers without saying which is which.
- Don't cite a price target or "consensus" without its source and date.
