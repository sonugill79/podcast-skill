# sources.md: the stone heat battery (FICTIONAL eval fixture)

Everything here is invented for the eval suite: the town, the lab, the people and every figure. The URLs use the
reserved `example.org` domain. Rows are numbered so a judge can cite them (`V3`, `R1`, `D1`).

Topic: how a silo of hot crushed rock stores spare wind power as heat for a town's district heating.

## Verified

| # | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | The Stonebridge unit is a steel silo twelve metres tall holding two thousand tonnes of crushed basalt, a hard volcanic rock | Westmere Energy Lab, *Stonebridge field report*, March 2025, §2 — https://example.org/westmere/stonebridge-2025 [primary] |
| V2 | It charges from wind power the local grid cannot use at that moment: electric coils heat air, and fans blow the hot air down through the stone, which reaches about six hundred degrees Celsius. To discharge, fans blow cooler air up through the stone; the hot air that comes out heats water for the town's pipes | same report, §2–3 [primary] |
| V3 | It holds about one hundred megawatt-hours of heat | same report, §2 [primary] |
| V4 | It lost about one percent of its stored heat per day in the 2025 winter trial | same report, §4 [primary] |
| V5 | About eighty-five percent of the electricity put in came back out as useful heat for the district heating network | same report, §4 [primary] |
| V6 | Turning the stored heat back into electricity would return under forty percent of the input (the report's estimate), so the unit sells heat only | same report, §5 [primary] |
| V7 | In January and February 2025 it supplied sixty percent of Stonebridge's district heating | same report, §4, table 3 [primary] |
| V8 | Quote, report lead Dana Ward: "Stone is boring, and boring is the point." | same report, foreword (checked verbatim) [primary] |
| V9 | **Misconception (everyday):** many people assume a "stone battery" gives electricity back, like a phone battery. It does not (V6) | Westmere Energy Lab FAQ, "Is it a battery?" — https://example.org/westmere/faq [primary] |
| V10 | **Misconception (practitioner):** the project's own engineers expected most of the daily loss to go through the silo walls; about two thirds of it went out through the air ducts at the top | same report, §4.2 [primary] |
| V11 | District heating: hot water piped from one central plant to every connected home | Westmere Energy Lab glossary — https://example.org/westmere/glossary [primary] |
| V12 | Scaling: a larger silo cuts wall loss per tonne of stone; it does not cut duct loss, which the report ties to the duct design | same report, §6 [primary] |
