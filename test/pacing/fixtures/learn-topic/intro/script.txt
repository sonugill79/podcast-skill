# ── 1. Heat kept in a silo of stone ─────
MAYA: I'm Maya, this is Alex, and today we're looking at the stone heat battery. Specifically, how a big tank of hot rock keeps a town warm.
ALEX: And I'm the one asking the questions today.
---
# ── 2. How a pile of rock holds heat ─────
MAYA: Here's what's at stake. A town wants to heat its homes in winter using wind power.
ALEX: Okay. So how do you keep wind power until someone needs it?
MAYA: Hold on to that. First, a smaller question. Sometimes the wind makes power the local grid can't use at that moment. That's spare power.
[pause 1.2]
MAYA: Now guess. Where does this town put its spare power?
ALEX: I don't know.
MAYA: Into rock. A steel tower called a silo, twelve metres tall, full of crushed basalt. Basalt is a hard rock made by volcanoes.
[pause 1.2]
ALEX: A tower full of crushed rock. How much?
MAYA: Two thousand tonnes. The spare power runs electric coils, which heat the air. Fans blow the hot air down through the stone.
ALEX: And the stone just... gets hot?
MAYA: Very hot. About six hundred degrees Celsius.
ALEX: Wait. [pause 0.7] Six hundred degrees? Inside a steel tower?
MAYA: Inside the steel, yes. Picture a grain silo, filled with crushed rock instead of grain.
[pause 1.2]
MAYA: Next guess. When the town needs heat, what comes back out?
ALEX: Electricity? Like a phone battery giving its charge back?
MAYA: That's the common guess. It isn't. Cooler air goes up through the stone. Hot air comes out. That hot air heats water for the town's pipes.
ALEX: Which pipes?
MAYA: District heating. That means hot water, piped from one central plant to every home on the network.
[pause 1.2]
MAYA: So it's like a giant flask of hot stones. Here's where that picture breaks. A flask only keeps heat in. This one lets you pull the heat out when you want it.
ALEX: Let me try it back. Spare wind heats the rock. The rock holds it. When it's needed, air carries the heat to the pipes.
MAYA: That's it. And that answers your puzzle. You keep the wind as heat, in rock. So, in one line. The stone is the store.
[pause 2]
---
# ── 3. What one winter showed ─────
MAYA: Now the part I think matters most to the town. Did it actually keep people warm?
ALEX: That's my question too. I'd want to know how much it leaks. How much does this lose?
MAYA: Guess first. In one day.
ALEX: I don't know. Tell me.
MAYA: About one percent a day, during a winter trial. By our arithmetic, after one day, ninety-nine parts in every hundred are still there.
ALEX: One percent. [pause 0.7] Only one?
MAYA: Only one. By our arithmetic, after a week more than nine parts in ten are still there.
[pause 1.2]
MAYA: Here's the big one. In January and February, it supplied sixty percent of the town's district heating.
ALEX: Sixty percent. From a pile of rock.
MAYA: Picture the town's piped heat in those two months as ten buckets of hot water. Six of them came from the stone.
MAYA: The report's lead, Dana Ward, put it like this. Quote. "Stone is boring, and boring is the point."
[pause 1.5]
ALEX: Let me try it back. It loses about one percent a day. And in January and February it ran most of the town.
MAYA: Most of the district heating, yes. Not the electricity. The report estimates that turning that heat back into power would return under forty percent. So it only sells heat.
[pause 2.5]
MAYA: That's why I think the word battery oversells it. My read? It's a heat store, and that's the honest pitch.
ALEX: I'd push back. It keeps spare power for later. I think that's what a battery is for.
MAYA: I'd say a battery gives you back what you put in. This gives you back something else.
ALEX: Fair. I'm not sure we settle that one.
MAYA: Maybe not. So, the recap. The stone is the store, and what it stores is heat.
---
# ── 4. What we couldn't verify ─────
ALEX: Before we go. What couldn't we pin down?
MAYA: Two things. A local paper reported the build cost four million dollars. We couldn't check that against the original records.
ALEX: Okay. And the second?
MAYA: A supplier's blog claims the stone lasts thirty years. That's their claim, not something we verified.
MAYA: So, three things to remember. The stone is the store. It loses about one percent a day. And it gives back heat, not power.
