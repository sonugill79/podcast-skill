# ── 1. The question ─────
HOST: I'm Iris, and today we're asking whether Ashgrove should replace its night buses with on-demand shuttles, city-wide. Making the case for is Marcus. Arguing the other side is Nora. My job is to keep them both honest, and to tell you at the end where the evidence actually points.
ADVOCATE: Happy to be here. I think this one is clearer than people admit.
SKEPTIC: And I think Marcus is about to overstate it.

# ── 2. What nobody disputes ─────
HOST: Start with the floor. Last year the city ran a one-year pilot. Three night bus routes in one district became on-demand shuttles. You book a van by app or phone. It picks you up at a corner near you, within four hundred metres.
[pause 1.2]
HOST: Shuttle waits averaged eleven minutes. The buses had run every thirty. Marcus, you'd accept those numbers?
ADVOCATE: I would. They're the city's own.
HOST: And the cost to the city. Fourteen dollars a trip by shuttle. Nine by bus.
ADVOCATE: Fourteen against nine. Fair. That's the hard number for my side.
HOST: Nora, you'd accept those numbers too?
SKEPTIC: I would. None of it is in dispute.
HOST: So that's the floor nobody here disputes. Eleven-minute shuttle waits, buses every thirty, and a more expensive trip for the city.
[pause 2]
---
# ── 3. The case for ─────
ADVOCATE: Iris, here's the case. I think thirty minutes at a bus stop at midnight is a long time. Shuttle waits averaged eleven minutes. The buses ran every thirty.
HOST: Eleven, against a bus every thirty.
ADVOCATE: And people came. Night ridership in the district rose twenty-two percent.
HOST: Wait. [pause 0.7] Twenty-two percent, in one year?
ADVOCATE: In one year. The pilot lead at the transit office, Sam Porter, put it like this. Quote. "Nobody should wait half an hour at midnight."
[pause 1.5]
HOST: So your case rests on the wait time?
ADVOCATE: On the wait, and on the ridership rise that came with it. Picture a bus stop at midnight, with thirty minutes on the clock. My read? That's what a night service is for. An eleven-minute wait, against a bus every thirty. That's the case.
[pause 2]
---
# ── 4. The case against ─────
SKEPTIC: Iris, my case starts with money. The shuttle cost the city fourteen dollars per trip. The bus cost nine.
HOST: Five dollars more per trip, by my arithmetic.
SKEPTIC: And look at who rode. Riders over sixty-five fell from fifteen percent of night riders to nine.
HOST: Hold on. [pause 0.7] Fifteen down to nine?
SKEPTIC: Fifteen to nine. And twelve percent of trips were booked by phone call. The other eighty-eight percent were booked in the app. I think the app is part of why. The report doesn't prove that.
SKEPTIC: The riders union's chair, Lena Park, put it simply. Quote. "A bus you can see is a bus you can trust."
[pause 1.5]
HOST: So your case rests on cost, and on who the service reaches?
SKEPTIC: Both. Picture a corner at midnight, and a rider with no app. Honestly, a night service has to work for the riders with no other way home. Fourteen dollars, not nine, and a smaller share of older riders. That's my case.
[pause 2]
